Roles¶

A role is what an agent is for: a description, instructions, the tools it may use and, if you like, its own model. Open Agents on the left rail ("Who does the work, in which teams, by which recipes"). The page has three tabs: Teams & roles, Recipes and History.

The roles¶

The Orchestrator is the agent you talk to. It opens other roles as sub-agents when a part of the work needs them. KOLONİ comes with these roles:

Orchestrator, Planner, Coder, Tester, Reviewer, Researcher, Debugger, Architect, Security reviewer, Docs writer, Refactorer, UI designer, Data analyst, DevOps, Spec writer, Performance engineer, Database engineer, Dependency keeper, Translator, Summarizer, Hive keeper, Scout, Data engineer, ML engineer, Data scientist, Model evaluator, MLOps engineer, Designer, SEO specialist, Copywriter, Accessibility reviewer.

Two are named for KOLONİ's hive. The Hive keeper keeps the To-do list: it turns a goal into tasks, each with what makes it done, and puts them in order. The Scout keeps watch on the To-do list: it splits vague tasks, asks what is missing, flags duplicates, stale and blocked tasks, and checks finished tasks against what makes them done. After each turn KOLONİ also asks a model, in the Scout's name, for follow-up tasks (see To-do). A role of your own that already had one of these names keeps it; the built-in one is then "Hive keeper (built-in)" or "Scout (built-in)". A slash command of yours named scout would hide behind the role's chip in the message box: it is renamed scout-command.

Before 0.1.7 they were the Product owner and the Assistant product owner. Your recipes and saved slash commands that named the old roles were changed to name the new ones, and an agent that asks for an old name is told the new one ("Product owner is now the Hive keeper"). Past conversations keep the names they had. A recipe kept in a folder's .koloni/recipes is a file of yours, not changed: one that names an old role is renamed there, by hand.

The Roles list on the Teams & roles tab shows them all. Filter roles… searches it, and the buttons All, In no team and Off narrow it. Each role has an on / off switch. The Orchestrator and the Planner are always on. So are the Coder, Tester, Reviewer, Debugger and Security reviewer: KOLONİ's checks and recipes use them, so they have no switch (a chat's teams still decide whether they are opened there).

In a window narrower than 1280 px the list folds to a Roles ▸ N button over the teams, which opens it as a drawer; it stays at the top of the page however far you scroll.

A role's panel¶

Click a role to open its panel. It has four tabs:

  • Overview: its category, the teams it is in, what it may do, and its Model as four answers: Which model, How hard it thinks, How much it reads and Who may move it (and Runs as where a role can run inside the CLI). Click an answer, or Edit, to change it.
  • Instructions: what the role is told.
  • Tools: the tools it may use, grouped (Files, Commands, Web, Browser, Team, To-do, Memory). A click switches a tool on or off and saves at once. A role needs at least one tool. The Coder, Tester, Reviewer, Debugger and Security reviewer keep the tools their job needs: reading files for all five, writing files for the Coder and the Debugger, Run commands for the Coder, the Tester and the Debugger. Those tiles stay on, greyed: "Kept: KOLONİ's checks and recipes need it". Their other tools are yours to change, and Reset to default still works. A sub-agent never has a tool its opener may not hand on: an Orchestrator without Run commands gives no Tester one. Tools marked "asks" wait for your yes, as the chat's permission level says. Below, the MCP servers and skills the role has; you change those on the Tools page.
  • Knowledge: a switch for each Knowledge topic. On, choose Full ("its whole guide at each step") or Map ("its guide, read when its work touches it").

Editing a role¶

Edit opens the role's form:

  • Name, and What it does: "The Orchestrator reads it to pick the role."
  • Category and Instructions.
  • Model: a card for each question. Each says one line; Learn more holds the rest.

Which model asks Who picks it?, then only what that choice needs:

Choice What it needs What it means
Orchestrator From and to "It picks a tier for each job, inside this range, and says why."
As the chat nothing "Whatever the chat's model picker says."
One tier Fast, Balanced or Strongest "Always this tier. The Orchestrator can't change it."
One model a Provider and a Model name "Always this model, whatever the job."

The Orchestrator itself has no Orchestrator choice. Try first lists up to 5 models from the team guide, tried in order before the choice above; + Add a model adds one. See Tiers.

How hard it thinks (Thinking) is Auto or One fixed level, and one ladder of the levels Off to Max:

  • Auto: the agent that opens a sub-agent picks a level for each task: Low for reading or summarizing, Medium for normal code, High for hard bugs or design. The levels it may pick are shaded, from Low up to the one marked most (High unless you move it, or the nearest lower level a model without High takes); click a level to move it. Its pick, and its reason, show under Details in the agent's window. Auto never picks Off. If it picks nothing, KOLONİ sends Medium (or the most, where that is lower), and the agent's window says "Thinking Medium, KOLONİ's default for Auto (the opener gave no level)".
  • One fixed level: every task gets the level you click, any level its model takes; the Orchestrator doesn't change it.

An agy model whose name ends in its level, such as gemini-3.8-flash-low, takes no other: it is sent none, and the agent's window says so. Under How hard it thinks, the role's Model card says "The model's own" and "its name sets its level: none is sent".

A role from before may still have a most next to a fixed level, or a most of Off under Auto. A line under the ladder says so ("The Orchestrator may also go up to High (set earlier)"), and Remove takes it away.

Struck levels are ones the role's model does not take, named under the ladder. A model without a level gets the nearest lower level it takes, never down to Off; one whose levels are not known gets none. Back to Auto, a most of Off, or one the model is known not to take, is taken away.

Nobody opens the Orchestrator, so it has no most: a click sets its level, and with Auto no level of its own is sent, and the chat's level, or the model's own, applies.

How much it reads (the context window) says the window found for its model, and where from. Past the % set in Settings › The app ("Summarise a long context at (%)") KOLONİ summarizes the older part, unless its CLI keeps its own session: Claude Code compacts its own; Copilot's and Codex's start anew past that % of their bound (Going on from the last turn). Where the window is unknown there is no fullness meter and nothing is summarized early - but a CLI lead that starts each turn anew, which is summarized after its turn against the catalogue's window for its model where there is one, which only a very long chat reaches (Set up a CLI). Set by hand takes a window of your own.

Who may move it: Any provider in the team guide, Only these, or No one. An agent that moves a sub-agent to another provider must say why.

Run inside the CLI: only when the agent that opens it runs in Claude Code or GitHub Copilot CLI run Native (experimental) - Copilot on its newer protocol (1.0.114 on), where its provider is in the team guide. Cheaper, and counts toward no turn or role limit; it is not its own agent on the canvas, stops with the agent that opened it, and is never used while you're away. Not for the Orchestrator or a team's lead. The agent's window and the canvas show it as an own sub-agent, "Checker (inside GitHub Copilot CLI)", with what it did inside it. It has its role's own tools only, each asked about as its agent's are (a card where your level says so): none of KOLONİ's tools, which are its agent's, and no sub-agent of its own. In Copilot it runs on the agent's own model; Copilot is given only these roles, none of its built-in sub-agents, and opens one only while it waits for it - one it asks to open in the background, by another name, or with a model, level or anything else of its own choosing, is refused. At Ask, a role that can change things is a card before it opens, as opening an agent is. - It may: Read and search, Write code, Run commands, Use the web, or Pick tools one by one. - This role will sums up what the role will do, as you change the form.

Press Save. A built-in role has Reset to default; your own has Delete role.

A new role¶

New role (on the Teams & roles tab) opens a form. "Agents in this role are opened by the Orchestrator or a team lead." Start from:

  • Blank;
  • An expert: it reads only; an expert changes nothing;
  • A copy of another role: it gets that role's servers, skills and knowledge, but not its teams.

Press Create role. An agent can also propose a role while it works; you decide on the Create this role? card.

History¶

The History tab shows what the roles, teams and recipes did in the Last 30 days or Last 7 days: how many agents were opened and ended done, and the team and recipe runs. Filter by role, team or recipe, or tick Failed only. Each row shows when, the role, the chat, the model and how it ended; click the chat to open it.
